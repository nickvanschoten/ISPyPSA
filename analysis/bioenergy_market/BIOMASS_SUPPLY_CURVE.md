# A stepped biomass feedstock supply curve — research basis, design, and implementation

**Date:** 2026-08-13. **Status:** implemented (config-gated, default off), unit-tested,
parameterised central case committed as
[`biomass_supply_curve_central.csv`](biomass_supply_curve_central.csv) (generated by
[`build_biomass_supply_curve.py`](build_biomass_supply_curve.py)). Smoke-validated
end-to-end (`bsc_smoke_nsw`, NSW 2040 rep-week, c550, gas + biomass curves both on,
HiGHS Optimal): the LP filled the zero-adder byproduct tranche exactly to its 30 PJ cap
and bought nothing at +$5.5/GJ — at the true marginal feedstock price biomass yields to
gas instead of over-building on residue-priced fuel; per-tranche usage lands in
`outputs/capacity_expansion_tables/biomass_supply_curve_usage.csv`, with the gas curve
metering independently on the same model. Shares the carrier-generalised
fuel-supply-curve machinery built for gas
([`GAS_SUPPLY_CURVE.md`](../gas_market/GAS_SUPPLY_CURVE.md)).

## 1. The problem: unlimited biomass at a flat residue price

The IASR prices biomass fuel at **$0.624/GJ, identical in every year 2022-23 → 2053-54
and in all three ISP scenarios, with no accompanying volume limit** (IASR v7.8 "Coal and
Biomass price" sheet, cached in this repo). That price is faithful for genuine on-site
byproducts (bagasse, black liquor — GenCost's own biomass fuel cost is ~$0.5-2/GJ), but
applied to an unlimited resource it makes biomass structurally the cheapest firm option:
the Phase 6 production run built 16.1 GW dispatching 135.9 TWh — **~70× current
Australian bioelectricity** (~1-3.2 TWh/yr from ~0.9 GW; Clean Energy Australia Report
2024: 992 GWh large-scale 2022-23, falling 8 % in 2023-24; ~3.2 TWh including
behind-the-meter sugar-mill cogeneration, Australian Energy Statistics 2021-22).

Two Pass-1 patches contained this: a NEM-wide capacity cap (1 → 5 GW by 2050,
[`_biomass_cap.py`](../archetypes/_biomass_cap.py)) and a flat feedstock re-price to
$6/GJ ([`_biomass_feedstock_cost.py`](../archetypes/_biomass_feedstock_cost.py)). The
flat re-price is conservative but loses the real shape — cheap residues run first, then
each increment costs more — and neither patch prices *volume*: below the cap, feedstock
is still perfectly elastic at one price. This curve is the rigorous fix (the "Option 2"
deferred at Phase 8.x), now cheap to build because the gas supply curve provides the
exact LP machinery (per-period tranche purchases + snapshot-weighted burn coupling).

## 2. Consistency with energy-economics practice

Every major framework that treats biomass as a first-class fuel uses a multi-step,
volume-bounded supply curve; AEMO's flat price is again the outlier:

| Model | Biomass fuel supply representation |
|---|---|
| **NREL ReEDS** | Regional (BA-level) supply curves in ~4 cost bins (urban wood waste/mill residues → forest residues → ag residues → energy crops), from POLYSYS / Billion-Ton Study ([ReEDS docs](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy21osti/78195.pdf)) |
| **US EPA IPM** | State/IPM-region price-quantity step curves, "similar to how coal is represented in the model", per-run-year, with explicit +$15/dry-ton transport and +$20/dry-ton storage on ag residues ([EPA Platform v6 Ch. 9](https://www.epa.gov/power-sector-modeling/documentation-ipm-platform-v6-all-chapters), Table 9-4, from DOE 2016 Billion-Ton) |
| **EIA NEMS** | Renewable Fuels Module biomass supply schedules over four feedstock categories, with feedstock *shared* between the electricity and liquid-fuels modules — cross-sector competition is endogenous ([EIA RFM docs](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/aeo/assumptions/pdf/renewable.pdf)) |
| **PyPSA-Eur** | Per-NUTS2-region volume caps by biomass category from JRC ENSPRESO (low/med/high availability scenarios), each category at its own cost — a de facto per-region step curve ([PyPSA-Eur sector docs](https://pypsa-eur.readthedocs.io/en/stable/sector.html)) |
| **TIMES/MARKAL** | Documented convention: split the resource into 3-4 stepwise potential groups, precisely because a bare LP otherwise exhausts the single cheapest source ([supply-curve literature](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/supply-curve)) |
| **AEMO ISP** | **Flat $0.624/GJ, all years, all scenarios, no volume cap** (IASR v7.8). The ACIL Allen fuel-price study for the 2026 ISP covers gas, liquid fuel, coal and renewable gas — biomass gets no bespoke market study |

Two further anchors: the EMF-33 model-comparison found that *calibration of residue
supply curves* was a major driver of divergence in bioenergy deployment across eleven
IAMs ([Daioglou et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-019-02539-x));
and AEMO's own input set already contains a three-step **biomethane cost-volume curve**
(ACIL Allen: landfill gas → AD of wastes → AD of crop residues, scenario-dependent
feasibility shares 90/70/45 % in Step Change), so stepped bio-feedstock supply is
already ISP-native — just not for the power sector's solid biomass.

## 3. Market basis for the central-case curve

Primary sources: [ARENA *Australia's Bioenergy Roadmap* — Resource Availability appendix](https://arena.gov.au/assets/2021/11/appendix-resource-availability-australias-bioenergy-roadmap.pdf)
(ENEA/Deloitte, Nov 2021); [ACIL Allen 2024 fuel-price projections for AEMO](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/major-publications/isp/2025/acil-allen-2024-fuel-price-forecast-report.pdf)
(biomethane cost-volume curves); [CSIRO/Boeing *SAF State of Play*](https://www.csiro.au/-/media/Science-Connect/Futures/Sustainable-Aviation/SAF-State-of-Play_CSIRO-Boeing.pdf)
(Nov 2024); Crawford et al. 2016, *GCB Bioenergy* 8(4); [IEA Bioenergy Australia country report 2024](https://www.ieabioenergy.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/CountryReport2024_Australia_final.pdf);
IRENA (2012) *Biomass for Power Generation* delivered-feedstock tiers (already sourced in
[`_biomass_feedstock_cost.py`](../archetypes/_biomass_feedstock_cost.py)). Prices real AUD/GJ.

Key facts each tranche rests on:

- **The resource is large but the cheap slice is small.** Theoretical potential
  >2,610 PJ/yr, of which the Roadmap's own modelling treats only ~45 % as
  economically/technically accessible; the ARENA cost curve prices waste streams
  negative-to-$0/GJ (levy avoidance), residues low-mid, and crop feedstocks up to
  $30-35/GJ. Current *total* bioenergy use is ~200-216 PJ/yr and has been flat since
  ~2010 (IEA Bioenergy 2024) — of it, bagasse (~100 PJ) and wood waste (~89 PJ) are
  mostly on-site process heat, not grid electricity.
- **Delivered residue cost ≈ $6/GJ.** CSIRO/Boeing price ag residues at ~$110/t and
  forest residues at ~$125/t (~$5.5-7/GJ at 18-20 GJ/t), consistent with IRENA's
  locally-collected tier ($4-8/GJ) and the existing flat-fix value of $6.0/GJ.
- **Competing sectors claim the same pools at higher willingness-to-pay.** AEMO/ACIL
  Step Change biomethane potential is ~250 PJ/yr post-2030 from exactly the cheap
  feedstocks (landfill gas, AD-suitable wastes, crop residues) — biomethane at gas
  parity (~$12-20/GJ) outbids power at residue prices. CSIRO/Boeing frame domestic
  feedstock as meeting 60 → 90 % of jet fuel demand by 2050; renewable diesel already
  exports canola and tallow. The power sector gets the *residual*, not the resource.
- **Supply chains are immature and localised.** ~100 km is the viable haul radius for
  raw biomass (Roadmap, citing Jacobs 2015); a QLD GIS-MILP study found a whole
  region's forest-residue chain supports ≤500 MW under a 105 km transport constraint,
  favouring 5-10 MW plants; the biogas fleet was 242 plants in 2017; the Roadmap's
  "biohub" aggregation concept remains at the 20 kt/yr pilot scale; ABARES projects
  **no new biomass-electricity investment by 2050 under current market conditions**.
  Sustainable-removal limits (retain 1-1.5 t/ha of residue for soil health) cap the
  collectible fraction below theory.
- **Energy crops don't exist yet at scale.** Crawford et al.: 70-80 % of the 2030/2050
  biomass expansion (to 100-115 Mt/yr) is *new short-rotation-coppice plantings*
  (14.7 Mt/yr by 2030, 29.3 Mt/yr by 2050 ≈ up to ~530 PJ) — a category with zero
  commercial deployment in Australia today and establishment-to-first-harvest lead
  times, priced by IRENA at $8-12/GJ.
- **The uncapped tail is import parity.** Internationally traded pellets are IRENA's
  top tier ($8-16/GJ delivered). Australia currently has no pellet-import chain, so
  ~$16/GJ delivered is a scarcity price for a market that would have to be built —
  the analogue of the gas curve's LNG-import anchor, but for a trade that does not
  yet exist in this direction.

### The central-case tranches

Adders are premiums above the IASR baseline (~$0.62/GJ, which the LP keeps as its
tranche-1 price — when this curve is on, the flat $6/GJ re-price pre-pass stands down).
Caps interpolate linearly between the anchor years shown; blank cap = uncapped.

| Tranche | Adder | Cap (PJ/yr) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| existing_industry_byproduct | +$0 | 30 flat | Today's fleet's burn: bagasse cogen + landfill gas + wood waste, ~1-3.2 TWh ≈ 15-50 PJ; genuine ~$0 byproducts at the IASR residue price |
| collected_residues | +$5.5 | 5 → 25 (FY2030) → 60 (FY2040) → 90 (FY2050) | Hauled forestry/sawmill/ag residues at CSIRO-Boeing delivered cost; power's residual claim after biomethane (~250 PJ ACIL Step Change) + SAF; ramp reflects Roadmap mobilisation barriers and ABARES' bearish base case |
| energy_crops | +$10 | 0 until FY2035 → 30 (FY2040) → 120 (FY2050) | Short-rotation coppice per Crawford et al.; zero industry today, first volumes late 2030s; 120 PJ ≈ a quarter of theoretical 2050 SRC; IRENA energy-crop tier |
| imported_pellet_backstop | +$15 | uncapped | ~$16/GJ delivered, top of IRENA traded-pellet tier; keeps the LP feasible while pricing extreme volumes prohibitively |

Properties: (i) at current-fleet volumes (≤30 PJ ≈ 1.7 TWh) **nothing changes** vs the
IASR baseline — existing bagasse/landfill units keep their genuinely-cheap economics
(fixing the flat re-price's known mis-pricing of the Tully unit); (ii) total
non-backstop supply is 35 PJ (2025) → 240 PJ (2050) ≈ **2 → 13.7 TWh at 17.5 GJ/MWh —
the curve lands the fleet inside the ~5-15 TWh AEMO/industry band by construction**;
(iii) at c550 in 2050 the marginal biomass MWh costs ~$28 (in tranche 1), ~$125 on
residues (≈ the flat-fix $122, preserving central-case continuity), ~$204 on energy
crops and ~$291 on the pellet backstop (fuel + residual-CH4/N2O carbon, ex-VOM) —
biomass grades from cheap niche to deep-residual instead of sitting at one price.

### Why stricter than gas

The gas curve's uncapped tiers reflect a mature, deep, arbitrage-linked market: LNG
diversion and import terminals really do supply hundreds of PJ at a knowable premium.
Biomass has no equivalent: no liquid market or reference price, a ~100 km haul economics
wall, collection chains that mostly don't exist (ABARES expects none to be built
organically), sustainability limits on removal rates, and three competing sectors with
higher willingness-to-pay. The curve therefore (i) starts its zero-adder tranche at
30 PJ vs gas's 110, (ii) ramps mobilisation slowly rather than stepping capacity in
whole terminals, (iii) gates energy crops on industry build-out (zero before FY2035),
and (iv) caps total non-backstop 2050 supply at 240 PJ vs the gas curve's ~1,050 PJ.

## 4. Formulation

Identical to the gas curve (see [`GAS_SUPPLY_CURVE.md`](../gas_market/GAS_SUPPLY_CURVE.md)
§4): per investment period, tranche purchase variables `q_t ≥ 0` in TJ/yr bounded by
caps; snapshot-weighted annual Biomass-carrier burn `Σ_g Σ_s w_s · HR_g · p_{g,s} ≤ Σ_t
q_t`; premiums `ipw_objective(y) · adder_t · q_t` in the objective. The machinery is the
carrier-generalised [`pypsa_build/fuel_supply_curve.py`](../../src/ispypsa/pypsa_build/fuel_supply_curve.py)
called once per configured fuel, so gas and biomass curves coexist on one model with
independent metering (unit-tested). This realises the "custom-constraints
weighted-energy-sum extension" the Option-2 sizing identified, without new framework
code. LP size impact: ~4 variables + 1 constraint per period.

Interaction with the existing pre-passes when the curve is configured:

- [`_biomass_feedstock_cost.py`](../archetypes/_biomass_feedstock_cost.py) **stands
  down** (logged): the IASR residue price stays in marginal costs as the tranche-1
  baseline; the curve carries the cost shape the flat price approximated.
- [`_biomass_cap.py`](../archetypes/_biomass_cap.py) (5 GW by 2050) **stays on** as a
  complementary capacity bound; the energy curve is now the binding constraint
  (240 PJ ≈ 13.7 TWh « 5 GW × 90 % CF ≈ 39 TWh), with the capacity cap guarding the
  build-rate channel the fuel curve doesn't see.

## 5. Implementation map

- Config: `biomass_supply_curve.curve_csv` (shared `FuelSupplyCurveConfig` in
  [validators.py](../../src/ispypsa/config/validators.py), default off — omitting the
  block changes nothing).
- Translator: [`fuel_supply_curve.py`](../../src/ispypsa/translator/fuel_supply_curve.py)
  validates the CSV (columns, per-period coverage, uncapped backstop) and emits the
  `biomass_supply_curve` pypsa-friendly table.
- LP: [`pypsa_build/fuel_supply_curve.py`](../../src/ispypsa/pypsa_build/fuel_supply_curve.py),
  called with carrier "Biomass" from `build_pypsa_network` after
  `_add_custom_constraints`, and re-added on the operational-stage model rebuild in
  `update.py`.
- Runner: `run_myopic.py --biomass-supply-curve <csv>` threads the path into every
  period config; `instrumented_runner.py` prints and saves
  `biomass_supply_curve_usage.csv` (per-period tranche fills in PJ + premium cost)
  alongside the gas usage table.
- Tests: `tests/test_model/test_fuel_supply_curve.py` (micro-LP solves parameterised
  over Gas/Biomass + a two-curve coexistence test showing premiums feed back into the
  dispatch merit order), `tests/test_translator/test_fuel_supply_curve.py`.

Usage:

```bash
uv run python analysis/benchmarks/run_myopic.py \
    --run-id bsc_c550 --periods 2025 2030 2035 2040 2045 2050 \
    --carbon-price 550 --tns-price 0 \
    --gas-supply-curve analysis/gas_market/gas_supply_curve_central.csv \
    --biomass-supply-curve analysis/bioenergy_market/biomass_supply_curve_central.csv \
    ... (other production flags as per the ccx chain)
```

## 6. Limitations and next steps

1. **NEM-wide curve.** Feedstock is *more* regional than gas (bagasse in QLD/northern
   NSW, forestry residues VIC/TAS) and ReEDS/IPM/PyPSA-Eur practice is regional. The
   100 km haul wall means a regional split is the highest-value refinement; the
   NEM-wide caps here are honest totals but allow spatial arbitrage the real chain
   can't do.
2. **Annual budgets only.** Bagasse is crushing-season-shaped and residue harvest is
   seasonal; a within-year profile is a second-order refinement (biomass is a small
   share of firm energy, unlike winter gas deliverability).
3. **Competing-use volumes are static assumptions.** Biomethane/SAF claims are netted
   out of the caps from published scenario numbers, not co-optimised. In Pass 2 the
   orchestrator prices cross-sector fuel competition itself — these caps should then
   be re-derived from the *gross* resource with competing demands made explicit.
4. **Sources to firm up**: the 992 GWh vs 3,190 GWh bioelectricity discrepancy
   (large-scale vs total incl. behind-the-meter — pull AES Table O for the definitive
   series); ACIL Allen state-level biomethane splits (PDF table extraction was
   unreliable; national totals are solid); Crawford et al. full text (paywalled;
   figures cross-checked via the Roadmap's citation of the same study).
5. **Validation discipline** (as for gas): after a re-solve, check the solved
   (volume, marginal adder) point lies on the assumed curve, compare fleet TWh
   against the 5-15 TWh band, and re-anchor tranche 1 if the CER/CEC actuals series
   moves.
