# A stepped gas supply curve for GPG — research basis, design, and implementation

**Date:** 2026-08-10. **Status:** implemented (config-gated, default off), unit-tested,
parameterised central case committed as [`gas_supply_curve_central.csv`](gas_supply_curve_central.csv)
(generated by [`build_gas_supply_curve.py`](build_gas_supply_curve.py)). Smoke-validated
end-to-end (`gsc_smoke_nsw2`, NSW 2040 rep-week, c550, HiGHS Optimal): the LP filled
existing_market 100 PJ → diverted_lng_spot 100 PJ → new_basin_development 133/150 PJ,
paying a $733M fuel premium, with the marginal molecule correctly priced at +$4/GJ;
per-tranche usage lands in `outputs/capacity_expansion_tables/gas_supply_curve_usage.csv`.

## 1. The problem: unlimited gas at a flat price

ISPyPSA buys gas at exogenous IASR prices (~$10–15/GJ, consultant forecasts) with no
volume feedback. The solved ccx frontier consumes, from 2040 on, **~460–640 PJ/yr of
gas for GPG at every carbon price** (CCS gas at high prices, unabated at low — the
carbon price swaps *which* gas, not *how much*). Against reality:

- Actual east-coast GPG consumption 2021–2025: **97–137 PJ/yr** (AEMO 2026 GSOO, Table 20).
- AEMO 2026 GSOO Step Change GPG forecast: low of ~61 PJ (2028), then **60–110 PJ/yr
  through 2050**.
- Total east-coast *domestic* consumption (all sectors) is ~500–530 PJ/yr, with supply
  capability **declining from ~1,900 to ~1,670 PJ/yr by 2030** and structural southern
  shortfalls from ~2030 (2026 GSOO §3.2, Table 1).

So the model's gas fleet burns ~5× AEMO's GPG outlook — roughly today's entire domestic
market — at a price that assumes none of that extra demand exists. A flat price is a
perfectly-elastic supply assumption; when the optimiser itself chooses gas demand, that
assumption understates the equilibrium price and over-builds gas at the margin. This is
the partial-equilibrium error the supply curve corrects.

## 2. Consistency with energy-economics practice

Every major planning framework in a liberalised market endogenises gas supply; AEMO's
ISP is the documented outlier:

| Model | Gas supply representation |
|---|---|
| **US EPA IPM** | Regional price-quantity **step supply curves** from ICF's Gas Market Model, iterated with the electricity LP until Henry Hub price and demand agree ([EPA Platform v6, Ch. 8](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2018-05/documents/epa_platform_v6_documentation_-_chapter_8.pdf)) |
| **NREL ReEDS** | Census-division supply curves: price responds to ReEDS gas demand around an AEO **reference price-quantity point**, elasticities regressed from 31 NEMS scenarios ([ReEDS documentation §3 + appendix](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy19osti/72023.pdf); [Cole, Medlock & Jani 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/eneeco/v60y2016icp486-496.html)) |
| **EIA NEMS** | Full gas-market QP (NGMM) exchanging seasonal regional prices with the electricity module until equilibrium ([EIA NGMM docs](https://www.eia.gov/analysis/handbook/pdf/NEMS_Natural_Gas_Market.pdf)) |
| **TIMES / MARKAL / MESSAGE** | "Multi-stepped supply curves are easily modeled… each step representing a certain potential of the resource available at a particular cost" ([TIMES docs](https://times.readthedocs.io/en/latest/part-1/01-introduction.html)); graded fossil resource supply curves ([MESSAGEix](https://docs.messageix.org/projects/global/en/latest/energy/resource/fossilfuel.html)) |
| **PyPSA-Eur** | Gas enters only at LNG terminals/pipeline entry points/extraction sites; volume-bounded biogas tranches compete at gas buses — a de facto supply curve ([PyPSA-Eur-Sec docs](https://pypsa-eur-sec.readthedocs.io/en/stable/supply_demand.html)) |
| **AEMO ISP** | **Fixed price trajectories** from a consultant's gas-market model (ACIL Allen GasMark for the 2026 cycle, Lewis Grey earlier) — the supply curve exists but lives outside the ISP; gas-electricity consistency is handled offline via the GSOO cycle ([ACIL Allen 2025 report for AEMO](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/gas/national_planning_and_forecasting/gsoo/2026/2026-acil-allen-2025-projections.pdf)) |

Australian evidence that the exogenous treatment fails at exactly our operating point:
Simshauser & Gilmore find NEM electricity-model gas-turbine burn projections
"incompatible with the outer operating limits of Australia's eastern gas market
functionality" ([AJARE 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-8489.70065)).
And AEMO's own IASR inputs already include **biomethane cost-volume curves** (ACIL
Allen), so tranche-style fuel supply is not foreign to the ISP input set.

Theory anchors: heterogeneous field costs ordered by cost give the upward-sloping
industry supply curve (Hotelling-style rent on inframarginal fields); in an
export-linked market the domestic price gravitates to **max(LNG netback, marginal
development cost)**, capped by **import parity (netback + regas)** once import
terminals exist — the explicit price-formation model of the ACCC gas inquiry
([LNG netback series](https://www.accc.gov.au/inquiries-and-consultations/gas-inquiry-2017-30/lng-netback-price-series)).

## 3. Market basis for the central-case curve

Primary sources: [AEMO 2026 GSOO](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/gas/national_planning_and_forecasting/gsoo/2026/2026-gas-statement-of-opportunities.pdf)
(Mar 2026); [ACCC Gas Inquiry Dec-2025 interim report](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/gas-inquiry-december-2025-interim-report.pdf)
and its [Rystad long-run cost-of-supply appendix](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/appendix-d-east-coast-gas-market-long-run-costs-gas-production-final-report.pdf);
[ACIL Allen 2025 projections for AEMO](https://www.aemo.com.au/-/media/files/gas/national_planning_and_forecasting/gsoo/2026/2026-acil-allen-2025-projections.pdf);
[ACCC LNG netback series](https://www.accc.gov.au/inquiries-and-consultations/gas-inquiry-2017-30/lng-netback-price-series).
All prices real ~Jun-2025 AUD/GJ.

Key facts each tranche rests on:

- **Marginal cost of domestic supply rises with volume and time** (Rystad for the
  ACCC): marginal source ~$6.8–7.3/GJ (2026) → $7.8–8.8 (2030) → **$13–14 (2035)** as
  2P depletes into 2C; 80% of 2P+2C point-forward ≤$9/GJ. Transport to southern city
  gates adds ~$1.3–4/GJ.
- **Export parity**: ACCC netback averaged ~$13.6 (2021), **~$42 (2022 crisis)**, ~$21
  (2023), ~$15–16.5 (2024–25); forward oil-linked netback $14.2 (yr 1) → $11.4/GJ (yr 5).
  Producer GSA offers for 2026–27 supply: $13.4–14.6/GJ.
- **LNG imports**: Port Kembla FSRU is built (500 TJ/d ≈ 180 PJ/yr); delivered cost
  ACIL ~$16–17 (incl. $1.50 regas), Rystad/Jemena $14–19, APA ~$23 at 2025 spot —
  a **$16–22/GJ** band.
- **New basins**: Narrabri ~$8.5–10 delivered (un-FID'd); Beetaloo-to-Sydney $14–18
  once trunk transport is included; ACIL's Step Change assumes Narrabri by 2030 and
  ~50 PJ/yr Beetaloo.
- **2022 natural experiment**: short-run supply is near-vertical above netback —
  southern spot hit the $40/GJ administered cap with no within-year supply response.
- **GPG demand band**: GSOO Step Change 60–110 PJ/yr; actuals 97–137 PJ (2021–25).

### The central-case tranches

Adders are premiums above the IASR baseline (tranche 1 buys at the baseline — that
price *already embeds* AEMO-consistent GPG volumes). Caps interpolate linearly between
the anchor years shown; blank cap = uncapped.

| Tranche | Adder | Cap (PJ/yr) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| existing_market | +$0 | 110 → taper to 90 by FY2045 | Top of GSOO GPG band at the IASR price; southern 2P decline, Longford closes 2033 |
| diverted_lng_spot | +$2 | 50 (FY2025), 100 from FY2026 | ~85 PJ/yr GSOO expected spot sales + Surat/Cooper increments, at oil-linked netback premium |
| new_basin_development | +$4 | 0 → 150 over FY2031–36 | Narrabri + Beetaloo-east + Qld 2C; Rystad 2035 marginal cost + transport |
| lng_imports | +$6 | 180 from FY2027; 360 from FY2033 | Port Kembla now, second terminal option; JKM netback + regas |
| redirected_export_csg | +$7 | 350 from FY2036 | LNG foundation contracts roll off ~2035+; new-well CSG + transport ≈ export/import parity |
| scarcity_backstop | +$18 | uncapped | Keeps the LP feasible at any volume; ~$30/GJ delivered, below the observed 2022 $40 administered cap |

Properties: (i) at AEMO-consistent GPG volumes (≤110 PJ) **nothing changes** vs the
committed frontier inputs; (ii) before ~2033 total non-scarcity supply is ~390 PJ/yr —
the honest statement of the GSOO 2029–2035 shortfall window; (iii) a ~535 PJ/yr
c550-2050 outcome stays feasible but its marginal molecules pay +$6/GJ (≈$19/GJ
delivered, ≈+$54/MWh on CCS-gas marginal cost), with an average uplift of ~$3.5–4/GJ
across the burn. The economics the flat price hid — Australia is arbitrage-linked to
world LNG in both directions, so the first-order correction is the **$12 → $18–20
level shift for large volumes**, not fine slope detail.

## 4. Formulation

ReEDS-style supply curve anchored at a reference point, implemented natively in
linopy (the structure TIMES calls a multi-stepped supply curve):

- Per investment period `y`, tranche purchase variables `q_t ≥ 0` (**TJ/yr** —
  originally GJ, which put tranche bounds at ~1e8 against a model otherwise
  spanning ~1e0–1e5; HiGHS warned of excessive bounds and cuPDLP-C terminated at
  iteration 0 with a spurious `Optimal` on an infeasible point on the rep-week
  NEM LP, 2026-08-13. TJ lands caps ~1e5, adders ~1e4, coupling coefficients
  ~1e-3–0.2 inside the model's range), `q_t ≤ cap_t` (uncapped backstop
  mandatory — validated at translate time).
- Coupling: `Σ_g Σ_s w_s · HR_g · p_{g,s} ≤ Σ_t q_t` over all Gas-carrier generators
  (weighted-energy-sum; `w_s` are snapshot weightings summing to 8760, so the budget
  is annual regardless of span-weighting; heat rates from the pypsa-friendly
  generators table, which includes carried recursive-dynamic vintages because the
  constraint is built post-injection).
- Objective: `+ Σ_t ipw_objective(y) · adder_t · q_t` — weighted exactly like every
  other operational cost, so `--span-weight-years` composes correctly.

Generator marginal costs keep their fuel-inclusive IASR baseline (and its regional
differentials); tranches price only the premium above it. The LP fills tranches in
adder order, yielding a convex piecewise-linear fuel cost. LP size impact: ~6
variables + 1 constraint per period — negligible; the known pitfall class is dual
degeneracy at step boundaries (documented for stepwise supply curves; acceptable for
a menu model, and adder separation of $1–2/GJ keeps steps distinct).

## 5. Implementation map

- Config: `gas_supply_curve.curve_csv` (shared `FuelSupplyCurveConfig` in [validators.py](../../src/ispypsa/config/validators.py), default off — omitting the block changes nothing).
- Translator: [`fuel_supply_curve.py`](../../src/ispypsa/translator/fuel_supply_curve.py) validates the CSV (columns, per-period coverage, uncapped backstop) and emits the `gas_supply_curve` pypsa-friendly table.
- LP: [`pypsa_build/fuel_supply_curve.py`](../../src/ispypsa/pypsa_build/fuel_supply_curve.py) — carrier-generalised since the biomass curve landed (see [BIOMASS_SUPPLY_CURVE.md](../bioenergy_market/BIOMASS_SUPPLY_CURVE.md)); called with carrier "Gas" from `build_pypsa_network` after `_add_custom_constraints`, and re-added on the operational-stage model rebuild in `update.py`.
- Runner: `run_myopic.py --gas-supply-curve <csv>` threads the path into every period config; `instrumented_runner.py` prints and saves `gas_supply_curve_usage.csv` (per-period tranche fills in PJ + premium cost) alongside the other capacity-expansion tables.
- Tests: `tests/test_model/test_fuel_supply_curve.py` (micro-LP solves: tranche filling order, premium in objective, per-period budget reset, span-weight scaling, no-fuel warning, missing-heat-rate raise, gas+biomass coexistence), `tests/test_translator/test_fuel_supply_curve.py`.

Usage:

```bash
uv run python analysis/benchmarks/run_myopic.py \
    --run-id gsc_c550 --periods 2025 2030 2035 2040 2045 2050 \
    --carbon-price 550 --tns-price 0 \
    --gas-supply-curve analysis/gas_market/gas_supply_curve_central.csv \
    ... (other production flags as per the ccx chain)
```

## 6. Limitations and next steps

1. **Annual budgets only.** Winter deliverability is the binding constraint in
   practice (GSOO assesses peak-day adequacy; NEMS/ReEDS price seasonally). A
   winter/peak-day cap is the natural second increment.
2. **NEM-wide curve.** Regional basis differentials stay in the IASR baseline prices,
   but a southern-shortfall-specific tranche structure (post-Longford) would be
   sharper. EPA/ReEDS practice is regional; first-order effect here is captured by
   the level shift.
3. **Biomethane blending** (scenario tables) is metered as gas by the constraint —
   conservative overstatement of natural-gas demand; zero in `cost_optimal` runs.
4. **Netback volatility dominates parameter uncertainty** (annual averages spanned
   $13.6–42/GJ in 2021–25); ±$4/GJ on tranches 2–5 is not conservative. A
   reservation-policy variant (Dec-2025 Gas Market Review) would *lower* tranche-5
   pricing. Sensitivity CSVs are one-line edits to `build_gas_supply_curve.py`.
5. **Validation discipline** (per ReEDS/EPA practice): after a re-solve, check the
   solved (volume, marginal adder) point lies on the assumed curve and benchmark
   against the ACCC netback series; re-anchor tranche 1 if AEMO's GPG band moves in
   the next GSOO.
